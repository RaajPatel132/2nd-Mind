Sleep well, every night

Sleep researchers keep finding the same thing: a cool, dark room and a regular bedtime matter more than any gadget. The habit people skip most is going to bed at the same hour every night, weekends included, and it is the one that pays back fastest.

Keep the bedroom a little cooler than the rest of the house and cover any light that cannot be switched off. A small, steady amount of light from a hallway or a charger is enough to lighten sleep, even when the sleeper never wakes up.

Caffeine is the quiet saboteur. A coffee at four in the afternoon can still be in the body at bedtime, and the effect is not only a slower start: the deep sleep of the first half of the night is shorter and lighter.

A short wind-down helps more than it sounds. Dim the lights an hour before bed, put the phone in another room, and do something slow. One study of shift workers found that a lavender pillow spray made no measurable difference, which is a useful reminder that routine beats remedies.

If you cannot sleep after twenty minutes, get up and sit somewhere dim until you feel sleepy again. Lying in bed watching the clock teaches the bed to mean worry, and the aim is for it to mean rest.
