# Where a recall turn's time goes

Recall is slower than its targets: the median is about 11 s from question to the end of the
answer, and the first token arrives near 10 s, against targets of 3 s (median) and 6 s (p95). This
page says where the time goes, so that the next change goes at the largest piece first. Nothing
here changes the pipeline; it is the measurement the change will be judged against.

It is worked out from a stored live run, never from logs
(`make eval-latency A=<run id>`), and it counts **fresh calls only**. A call replayed from the
harness's response cache costs no time and says nothing about a provider, so the table says how
many calls it left out, and a run that was mostly replayed would show it.

## Reading it

- **The steps wait for each other.** Intent, the plan, the query embedding, the rerank and the
  answer run in that order, so their medians add. Two of them are about a third each: the **rerank**
  and the **plan**. Intent is under a fifth and the answer about a seventh.
- **The plan is where the slow turns come from.** Its median is 2.8 s but its p95 is 8.2 s: a few
  plans take three times as long as most, and they set the run's p95 of 17 s.
- **The rerank is mostly writing.** It spends about a second before it starts and then about 5 ms
  for every token it writes, and it writes a score and a one-line reason for each of 20
  candidates. Its time is its output.
- **The embedding is not the problem:** half a second.

## What would shorten it, and what each would cost

None of these is done. Each changes what a model is asked, or when, so each needs a live recall
run against the quality targets (hit@5 of 0.85 overall and 0.75 for every shape of question)
before it ships. Roughly in order of what the data above supports:

1. **Write less in the rerank.** About 370 tokens a call at 5 ms each is close to 2 s of the 2.9 s.
   Most of the output is the reason text for each candidate; scores alone would be roughly half the
   tokens, which is about a second saved per turn by the fit above (an estimate, not a
   measurement). The cost is the reason lines the Retrieval panel shows, so it is also a decision
   about the glass box: for example, reasons only for what gets selected.
2. **Judge fewer candidates.** The rerank always sees the top 20. In 6 of the 67 calls above, a
   candidate worth keeping sat below rank 10 (situational, counting and time-window questions,
   mostly), so cutting to 10 would have changed those turns. Cutting is not free; it is a trade to
   measure.
3. **Start the plan and the intent together.** Intent is a sixth of the chain. Running it beside
   the plan hides it on recall turns, and pays for a plan that a save turn throws away. The
   discarded call has to be on the usage ledger like any other.
4. **Look at the plan's tail.** Why a few plans take 8 s is not answered here: the stored runs keep
   the plan's output, not its reasoning time.
5. **Skip the rerank when it cannot matter** is smaller than it sounds. Only 4 of the 67 calls
   judged a single candidate (the case where ranking has nothing to order), and in all four the
   candidate scored well, so the skip would have saved a call without changing an answer. That is
   about 6% of the calls.

What does not help: prompt caching. Haiku 4.5 caches only prefixes of 4,096 tokens or more, and
the core-memory prefix is at most about 1,500
([ADR-0031](adr/0031-economy-routing-from-measurement.md)).

## Measuring again

A profile in which every call is fresh costs about thirty cents for the 51 cases
(`make eval-recall-live ARGS=--no-cache`, inside a live budget). Until then the run above is the
evidence, and the
[model-routing case study](case-studies/model-routing.md) explains how it was made and what it
does not show.
