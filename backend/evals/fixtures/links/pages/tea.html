A short guide to oolong

Oolong sits between green and black tea, because the leaves are only partly oxidised before they are fired. That is why one oolong can taste of orchids and another of toasted chestnut. Lightly oxidised ones are floral and bright, while heavily roasted ones are dark, warm and slightly sweet.

Use water just off the boil and a generous measure of leaf, then brew short, hot infusions and keep going. A good oolong will give five or six cups, and the flavour usually changes from one to the next, so the third cup is often the best.

Store the leaves in an airtight tin away from light, and keep them apart from anything with a strong smell, because tea takes up other aromas quickly.
